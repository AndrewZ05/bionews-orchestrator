# 60-Second Verbal Explanation Script
## A Disciplined Approach to Identity

---

### The Script (Memorize or paraphrase)

---

**Opening (10 seconds):**

"Product is asking for an identity system they can trust — one that's deterministic, auditable, and doesn't change history. That's what supports reporting, analytics, and client trust."

---

**The Tension (15 seconds):**

"Marketing, on the other hand, needs identity to *perform* — better reach, better attribution, better revenue outcomes. Those needs are inherently probabilistic.

The problem is: these requirements conflict directly."

---

**The Mistake (10 seconds):**

"The mistake most companies make is forcing one identity model to do both jobs. That either breaks trust or limits growth."

---

**The Solution (15 seconds):**

"Our approach separates identity by intent. We keep a deterministic system of record for truth — that's what Product needs. And we layer probabilistic identity on top for marketing — without letting one contaminate the other."

---

**The Payoff (10 seconds):**

"That's how we get both accuracy and performance. And that's why this architecture also becomes the foundation for a future CDP."

---

### Handling Common Pushback

**"Why are we making this so complicated?"**
> "We're not adding complexity — we're acknowledging reality. Different uses have different requirements. Pretending they don't is what creates real complexity later."

**"Why not just pick one identity?"**
> "Because one identity can't safely serve both reporting and marketing. We'd either break trust or limit growth. This approach gives us both."

**"Why not use a vendor CDP?"**
> "Vendor solutions often overwrite history and produce opaque decisions. Our architecture preserves evidence, making every decision defensible."

**"Won't the marketing layer be slow at scale?"**
> "It scales with identity complexity, not event volume — and we prune weak edges early. Runtime is bounded and predictable."

---

### The One Line to Remember

> **"We don't choose between accuracy and reach. We choose where each one belongs."**
